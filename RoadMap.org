* Scanning
** DONE Offline OCR pipeline with a debug lab
CLOSED: [2026-11-06 Fri]
All on the device:
1. OpenCV finds the receipt (by its corners, or the bright region), crops,
   deskews, evens out the lighting and binarizes.
2. Tesseract reads the text, with word positions and confidence.
3. Words are grouped into rows and handed to the parser.

The debug lab shows every stage and has switches for each step, to tune the
pipeline on real photos.
** DONE Single-column layout using the engine's own lines
CLOSED: [2026-11-07 Sat]
Receipts are one narrow column. Telling Tesseract so, and using its own line
grouping instead of rebuilding rows from word positions, fixed most split and
merged rows.
** DONE Parse rows into items, discounts, total and date
CLOSED: [2026-11-07 Sat]
Rule-based, no AI model. It finds the column header, item lines with their
prices, discount lines, the total and the payment lines, and the date. The total
is checked against the sum of the items, which lets it fix a single misread price.
** DONE Prefill a new receipt from a scan
CLOSED: [2026-11-07 Sat]
"Use this receipt" turns a scan into a new receipt ready to review. The store is
matched by the tax ID printed on the receipt, or by name.
** DONE Weights of scanned items from purchase history
CLOSED: [2026-11-08 Sun]
Most Italian receipts don't print weights. If the same product was bought before
in the same store at the same unit price, its weight is filled in. Never applied
to saved receipts.
** DONE Store tax IDs beyond Italy
CLOSED: [2026-11-09 Mon]
The parser was made generic: year-first dates, Spanish payment words, change
lines not taken as totals, and several tax ID formats.
** DONE Optional erasing of long lines and creases
CLOSED: [2026-11-09 Mon]
Removes ink strokes much longer than any letter before binarizing, so folds and
creases don't break lines. Lab only, off by default.
** DONE Try other layout modes when a receipt doesn't add up
CLOSED: [2026-11-09 Mon]
The chosen mode runs first; only if the result doesn't add up are the others
tried. Readings are ranked by:
1. Items that add up to the total, with no corrections.
2. More product names you already have, because names matter as much as amounts:
   they link items to products, aliases, comparisons and stats.
3. Confidence of the name words.
4. More items.

** DONE Match misread product names to known products
CLOSED: [2026-11-22 Sun]
A scanned name one or two letters away from a product you already have is
corrected to it. Rules to stay safe:
- At most two letters, and fewer for short names (four letters or less: exact
  only).
- Digits must be identical, so "500 g" never becomes "250 g".
- If two products are equally close, nothing is changed.
** DONE Spanish receipts: printed weights, units column, VAT table check

- A units column left of the names ("1 CANÓNIGOS") is detected and used as the
  quantity.
- Unlabelled Spanish CIF with its check character.
- The VAT summary table (base + tax) cross-checks the total, which helps when the
  total and the card payment are printed in the same large, hard-to-read font.

** DONE Camera photos rotated sideways
CLOSED: [2026-11-28 Sat]
With the phone held flat over a receipt on a table, the camera followed the
motion sensor, flipped to landscape and tagged the photo with a 90° or 270°
rotation that OpenCV couldn't undo and that badly hurt OCR. The capture rotation
is now pinned to portrait, matching the portrait-only UI. Tap to focus, pinch to
zoom and the torch work as before. Gallery photos still follow their EXIF
rotation.

Unit tests cover everything after OCR, but preprocessing and Tesseract have no
automated tests: a preprocessing change can silently break receipts that read
fine today. Instrumented tests should run the whole pipeline on stored photos
(personal data blanked) and check the expected items and prices. Needed before
the next real preprocessing change.
** HOLD Line and crease eraser in regular scanning

** IDEA Column-based reading

* Backups
** DONE Turn off system backup for now
CLOSED: [2026-11-28 Sat]
With system backup on, Android uploads the app database to the phone vendor's
cloud. That contradicts "your data stays on your phone", so it's off until there
is a real backup design. Phone-to-phone transfer still works.
** HOLD User-controlled backups
Being studied before 1.0. The app never sends your data anywhere; you choose where
copies go. Losing everything when a phone breaks is a real problem, so some form
of backup is needed. Must be decided before release: changing the rules after
users already have cloud copies is messier.
*** IDEA Export and import a backup file, optionally password-protected
Through the system file picker, like many privacy-focused apps do.
*** IDEA Automatic copies to a folder you choose
Periodic copies to a folder picked once, keeping the last few. If that folder is
synced (Nextcloud, Syncthing or anything else), that's the user's choice.
*** IDEA Opt-in system cloud backup, off by default
For those who want it. Possible with framework APIs only: a custom backup agent
can check the setting at backup time and skip the database, and can tell cloud
backups from phone-to-phone transfers and whether they are end-to-end encrypted.
Phones without Google services don't benefit anyway.
